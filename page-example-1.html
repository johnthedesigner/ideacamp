A predictive analytics tool, from zero to shipped

Founding design for a new analytics product: the first concept, the system beneath it, and the flows that put forecasting in customers' hands.

Role0→1 product design lead
ScopeDirection, system, 5 core flows

The problem

The team had a forecasting engine and no product around it. Analysts trusted the numbers; everyone else met a wall of configuration. The job was to turn a capability into something a non-analyst could open on a Monday and act on by lunch.

There was no design system, no shared language for charts, and no agreement on what "confidence" should look like on screen. Every decision compounded, so the foundation had to come first.

The approach

I started with the smallest honest version of the product and designed outward. Direction and a token foundation in week one, so color, type, and spacing stopped being debated per screen. Then the core flows, each specced tightly enough that engineering could build without a meeting.

The forecast was never the hard part. Making a busy person believe it, in three seconds, was.

Confidence became the spine of the interface: a single, accessible color scale for certainty, reused everywhere a prediction appeared. Because it was tokenized, one adjustment tuned the whole product.

What shipped

A product a first-time user could read without training: a forecast view, a driver breakdown, and an alerting flow, all sharing one system. The handoff included the tokens, the component scaffold, and written rationale for the decisions that mattered.

What carried over
