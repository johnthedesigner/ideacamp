# Ideacamp.co — Website Brief

**Project:** Marketing site for Ideacamp, a productized design studio, plus a lightweight intake form.
**Domain:** ideacamp.co

**How to read this brief:** It defines intent, audience, content, and voice. It deliberately does not prescribe layout, visual style, typography, color, imagery, component patterns, or page structure. Those are the designer's decisions, and the designer is expected to make strong, opinionated ones. Where this brief lists content, treat it as an inventory to be organized and presented however best serves the goals, not as a wireframe. The only hard requirements are marked as constraints in Section 6.

---

## 1. What Ideacamp is

Ideacamp sells fixed-scope, fixed-price design engagements, not hours and not retainers. Every offer has a defined deliverable, a defined timeline, and a defined amount of client involvement. The studio is run by one senior designer (14+ years: founding designer at an AI training platform, product design lead on a 0→1 predictive analytics tool at Tableau, deep design systems and token pipeline expertise).

The operating model the site must get across, confidently and without over-explaining:

- **Async-first.** Work is delivered through written docs, recorded walkthroughs, and working files. Progress doesn't depend on meetings.
- **Bounded sync.** Live calls happen in scheduled windows (Wednesdays and Fridays). Some offers require zero meetings. This is framed as a deep-work delivery model that produces better work faster, never as a limitation.
- **Deliberately limited capacity.** One or two active engagements at a time. This should read as quality control, not busyness.

The site itself is proof of craft. It will be judged as a design systems specialist's own storefront by an audience that notices typographic and structural discipline. How to demonstrate that craft is the designer's call.

## 2. Audience

Primary, in order:

1. **Seed/pre-seed founders** who need founding-designer judgment but can't hire one. They skim, they're busy, they buy outcomes and certainty. Price and timeline clarity is the conversion lever.
2. **Agency and consultancy principals** (dev shops without design capacity, design studios needing senior overflow) evaluating Ideacamp as a white-label/bench partner. They buy reliability and narrowness of specialty.
3. **Design/product leaders** at small-to-mid B2B companies with a specific systems problem (tokens, theming, dataviz color).

All three should find their entry point quickly, without the site fragmenting into three separate pitches.

## 3. Voice

First person singular where it matters ("I've spent 14 years..."), studio-neutral elsewhere. Punchy, short sentences. Confident, concrete, zero hype. No exclamation points. No em dashes; use commas or periods. Banned vocabulary: "elevate," "unlock," "supercharge," "world-class," "passionate," "seamless," "journey." Every claim should be checkable or scoped.

## 4. Content inventory

What follows is the substance the site must contain. Order, grouping, emphasis, and presentation are open.

### 4.1 Core message
Senior, founding-designer-caliber design delivered as products: defined deliverables, defined timelines, defined prices, async-first. Sample headline directions the designer may use, adapt, or discard: "Fixed scope. Fixed price. Design that ships." / "Senior design, packaged like a product." / "Design systems and 0→1 product design, in fixed-scope engagements."

### 4.2 How an engagement works
The funnel is deliberately async: a short intake form (ten minutes, no meeting) → a written scope, fixed price, and start date within 2 business days, with an optional 30-minute call in a Wednesday/Friday window → async build with working files, written rationale, and recorded walkthroughs so the client always knows where things stand → handoff with documentation and a revision window included. The primary conversion action everywhere is starting an intake, not booking a call.

### 4.3 The offers
Each offer must communicate: what it promises, what's delivered, how long it takes, how much of the client's time it needs, its meeting policy, and its price. This transparency is the product's differentiator; how it's presented is open.

**A. Design System Audit + Roadmap** — the entry offer
- Promise: know exactly what's wrong with your design system and what to fix first.
- Deliverables: written audit (tokens, components, accessibility, workflow), prioritized roadmap, recorded walkthrough.
- Timeline: 2 weeks. Client time: ~1 hour total, async. Meetings: none required.
- Price: $3,500

**B. Brand-to-Token Pipeline**
- Promise: your brand, converted into a production token system your engineers will actually use.
- Deliverables: DTCG-format token architecture, Style Dictionary pipeline with platform outputs, Figma variables setup, accessible color scale system, docs.
- Timeline: 2–3 weeks. Client time: kickoff + handoff, two calls max (Wed/Fri windows).
- Price: $7,500

**C. Design System Foundation Sprint**
- Promise: the foundation a design system needs to scale: tokens, type, color, core component scaffolding, and the documentation to maintain it.
- Deliverables: full token set and pipeline, accessible color system (light/dark), type and spacing scales, core component scaffold, contribution/maintenance docs.
- Timeline: 3–4 weeks. Client time: weekly 30-minute check-in (Wed/Fri) plus async reviews.
- Price: $12,500

**D. 0→1 Product Foundation** — the founding-designer offer
- Promise: founding-designer judgment for your first product: direction, system, and core flows, handoff-ready.
- Deliverables: visual direction, design system foundation, 3–5 core product flows designed and specced, engineering handoff package.
- Timeline: 4–6 weeks. Client time: weekly check-in (Wed/Fri) plus async decisions within 24h.
- Price: $18,000

**Add-on: Data Visualization Color System.** Accessible categorical and sequential chart palettes, CVD-verified, sequenced, delivered as tokens. $3,500 attached to B or C, $4,500 standalone.

**Agency note (must appear somewhere near the offers):** white-label and overflow engagements are available in these same shapes; mention it in the intake.

### 4.4 Why the model works
Three ideas, expressed however works: scope you can see (no discovery-phase black hole; price and finish line known before start); async by default (written rationale and recorded walkthroughs beat status meetings; the client's calendar stays theirs); systems thinking (everything ships with the structure to maintain it: tokens, docs, pipelines).

### 4.5 Proof
- Credibility: 14+ years in product design; founding designer experience in AI products; led 0→1 design of a predictive analytics tool at Tableau; deep specialization in design systems, token architecture (DTCG, Style Dictionary), and accessible color systems.
- Two case study teasers (title, one-sentence outcome, link stub for future detail pages): the Tableau 0→1 story and a design systems/token pipeline story.
- Space for 2–3 testimonials, designed to degrade gracefully while none exist at launch.

### 4.6 Questions worth answering on the page
How meetings work (sync windows Wednesdays and Fridays; several offers need none). What if a project doesn't fit an offer (intake anyway; scoping is free, including an honest "this isn't a fit"). What's needed to start (access, brand assets, a decision-maker who responds within 24h). Revisions (one structured round per offer, defined in the scope doc). Who does the work (one senior designer, no juniors, no outsourcing). Agency/white-label (yes; same offers, your client relationship).

### 4.7 Site-wide
Ideacamp as the brand name throughout. The intake action reachable from anywhere. A contact email. Social links are not required at launch.

## 5. Design latitude

The designer owns: page structure and count, information hierarchy, visual identity, typography, color, imagery approach, motion, component patterns, and how the offers and their transparency are expressed. The intent to serve: precise, quietly confident, craft-forward; a studio a design systems engineer would inspect and respect, and a busy founder could buy from in under three minutes. Distinctiveness is welcome; the brief's restraint on visuals is delegation, not a request for blandness.

## 6. Constraints (the actual hard requirements)

- Content and claims as specified in Section 4; prices displayed, no hourly rates anywhere, no urgency mechanics.
- The owner's name may appear in the proof content but the brand is Ideacamp, not a personal feed. No mention of current or day-job employment anywhere.
- Accessible (WCAG AA minimum), fast, responsive.
- Launch scope is intentionally small: the marketing page(s) plus intake form. No blog, newsletter, or chat.
- Built cleanly enough to survive inspection by a design systems engineer (semantic structure, tokenized styling), because it will get inspected.

## 7. Pricing rationale (context for the owner, not site copy)

Prices are positioned mid-pack for senior solo/boutique providers, July 2026 market:

- **Audits:** senior freelancer flow/heuristic audits run ~$2,000–8,000; agencies charge $8,000–25,000 for full-scope; a budget fixed-scope tier exists around $1,000–1,800. At $3,500, Offer A sits mid-range for a senior specialist while staying an easy first purchase, and clearly above the commodity tier.
- **Design systems:** freelancer-built design systems run ~$5,000–20,000; agencies $10,000–50,000. Offer C at $12,500 is the middle of the freelance band; Offer B at $7,500 prices the token pipeline as a defined slice of that work, justified by rare DTCG/Style Dictionary specialization.
- **0→1 product design:** early-stage app design typically lands $15,000–35,000 with mid-tier agencies and $8,000–40,000 freelance. Offer D at $18,000 is mid-market for senior solo work with a systems deliverable included.
- **Model comps:** async productized studios charge ~$6,000/month subscriptions for one-request-at-a-time senior design with recorded-walkthrough delivery; full-service agency retainers start around $15,000–25,000/month. Ideacamp's fixed-scope model should be framed against these: comparable seniority, no subscription commitment.
- Senior specialist market rates ($100–200/hr equivalent) mean each offer should be scoped so effective rate stays at or above that band as delivery speeds up with reuse.

Prices may be revised after the first 2–3 engagements or based on agency-channel intel. Never display hourly math on the site.

## 8. Success criteria

A seed founder can land, understand the model, pick an offer, and submit an intake in under three minutes without ever needing a call. An agency principal can identify the white-label path quickly. A design systems engineer can inspect the site and nod.
