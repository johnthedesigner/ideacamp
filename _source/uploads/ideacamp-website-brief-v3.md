# Ideacamp.co — Website Brief (v3)

**Project:** Marketing site for Ideacamp, a productized design studio. One main page, one supporting "engagement details" page, one intake form.
**Domain:** ideacamp.co

**How to read this brief:** It defines intent, audience, content, and voice. It deliberately does not prescribe layout, visual style, typography, color, imagery, component patterns, or how sections are visually expressed. Those are the designer's decisions, and strong, opinionated ones are expected. Content listed here is an inventory to organize in whatever way best serves the goals; the section order given is the intended narrative order, but its expression is open. Hard requirements are only in the Constraints section. One exception where interaction intent IS specified: the hero selector, whose behavior matters to the business model (its visual and interaction design remain open).

---

## 1. What Ideacamp is

Ideacamp sells senior design work as fixed-scope, fixed-price engagements — not hours, not retainers. There are exactly three engagements, three sizes of one thing: getting a product's design foundations right. Every engagement has a defined deliverable, timeline, price, and amount of client involvement, all visible before anyone commits.

The studio is one senior designer: 14+ years in product design, founding designer at an AI training platform, product design lead on a 0→1 predictive analytics tool at Tableau, deep specialization in design systems, design tokens and build pipelines, and 0→1 product design.

Operating model to convey confidently, without over-explaining: work is delivered async-first through working files, written rationale, and recorded walkthroughs, with a short written update every week — the client always knows where things stand and progress never waits on a meeting. Capacity is deliberately limited to one or two engagements at a time; this should read as quality control.

The site itself is proof of craft: it will be judged as a design specialist's own storefront by an audience that notices typographic and structural discipline. How to demonstrate that is the designer's call.

## 2. Audience and what they're worried about

1. **Seed/pre-seed founders (primary).** Buying an outcome and certainty about cost and time. Afraid of open-ended engagements, hiring wrong, and being unable to evaluate design quality. Convinced by a visible price and finish line, the actual person's track record, and a fast, low-effort start. Repelled by vagueness, agency-speak, and sales processes. They care nothing about our working methods except as proof of their certainty and visibility.
2. **Agency/consultancy principals** evaluating Ideacamp as a white-label bench partner. Buying risk reduction: reliability, polish, zero management. The site's job for them is verification, not conversion — they arrive from outreach.
3. **Design/product leaders** with a specific systems problem (tokens, theming, consistency). Buying depth. Convinced by precise domain vocabulary and deliverables their engineers will respect.

Message hierarchy, fixed: (1) certainty — fixed price, visible finish line; (2) seniority — the 14-year specialist does all the work; (3) durability — everything ships with the system to maintain it; (4) visibility — you always know where things stand. Number 4 supports number 1 and is stated confidently exactly once in full; it is never framed as a meetings policy or availability limitation. No scheduling specifics (days, windows) appear anywhere on the site.

## 3. Voice

First person singular where accountability matters ("I've spent 14 years…"), studio-neutral elsewhere. Short sentences. Confident, concrete, zero hype. No exclamation points. No em dashes; use commas or periods. Banned words: "elevate," "unlock," "supercharge," "world-class," "passionate," "seamless," "journey." Every claim checkable or scoped. Never imply Ideacamp is more than one person ("studio" is fine; a fictional "we/team" is not). No urgency mechanics, no manufactured social proof, no hourly rates anywhere.

## 4. The hero: a problem selector

**Intent (specified):** The first thing a visitor meets, after a kicker naming the specialization and one certainty line, is the question "What's in the way?" with six choices — the five problems below plus "Something else." Choosing one reveals the recommended engagement, its price, one line of why, and a link into the intake with that problem pre-filled. "Something else" links straight to the intake's free-text field.

**Requirements:** The selector accelerates but never gates — the full engagement content exists as plain, scannable page content below, and the page must work completely without the selector (no-JS, screen readers, skimmers). The interaction should read as precision, not gimmick; if in doubt, simpler.

**Open to the designer:** the selector's entire visual and interaction form.

**The five problems, verbatim (this language is decided content):**
1. "We're shipping fast with AI tools, and the product's starting to look like five different apps."
2. "We're raising, or selling to enterprises, and the product doesn't look like a company you'd wire money to."
3. "There's no designer, so every engineer invents their own UI as they go."
4. "The rebrand is approved. Now someone has to make it real in a codebase where colors live in forty places."
5. "People sign up and then vanish, and the flows are probably why."

Routing: 1 → Diagnose (leading to Build the Foundation) · 2 → Design the Product · 3 → Diagnose or Build the Foundation · 4 → Build the Foundation · 5 → Diagnose (leading to Design the Product).

## 5. Content inventory, in narrative order

### 5.1 Verification strip
Fast credibility scan: 14+ years · founding designer, AI products · led 0→1 design of a predictive analytics tool at Tableau · one or two engagements at a time. Presentation open.

### 5.2 The engagements
Framing idea: "Three sizes. Every engagement is one of them." Each engagement presents: what it promises, deliverables, timeline, the client's time cost, and price. This transparency is the differentiator; its presentation is open.

**Diagnose — $3,500 — 2 weeks.** Your product's design, examined: what's broken, what to fix, in what order, plus a fixed quote for the fix. Deliverables: written findings, prioritized roadmap, recorded walkthrough, follow-on quote. Your time: about an hour, all async. Credit note (must appear with this engagement): the fee credits in full toward either larger engagement if booked within 60 days — link to details page.

**Build the Foundation — $7,500–12,500 — 2–4 weeks.** The system engagement, configured to the situation: design tokens and build pipeline · accessible color and theming (light/dark) · component foundations and AI-readable documentation. Price fixes within the band in the written scope. Deliverables: the configured system, working pipeline, and the documentation to maintain it. Your time: a short weekly check-in plus async reviews.

**Design the Product — $18,000 — 4–6 weeks.** The founding-designer engagement: visual direction, the design foundation, and 3–5 core product flows designed and specced for engineering handoff. For new products, fundraise-driven overhauls, and flow problems that go deeper than a fix. Your time: a short weekly check-in plus async decisions within a day.

Close of section, small: "Agencies: I take white-label and overflow engagements in these same shapes. Mention it in the intake."

### 5.3 How buying works
Four steps: (1) A short intake form. Ten minutes, no meeting. (2) Within 2 business days: a written scope, a fixed price, and a start date. If you'd rather talk it through first, a 30-minute call is there for the asking. (3) Async build: working files, written rationale, recorded walkthroughs, a short written update every week. (4) Handoff with documentation and a revision round included. Primary conversion action everywhere: start an intake. Never "book a call."

### 5.4 Why it works this way
The model's single full statement, four ideas: **Scope you can see** (price and finish line in writing before work begins) · **You always know where it stands** (working files, written rationale, recorded walkthroughs, weekly written update; progress never waits on a calendar) · **Senior hands only** (one or two projects at a time, all work by the same specialist, no juniors, no handoffs) · **Built to last** (ships with the tokens, docs, and pipelines to maintain it).

### 5.5 Who you're working with
First-person bio: the credentials from Section 1, specialization list (design systems, token architecture — DTCG, Style Dictionary — accessible color systems, 0→1 product design). Two case-study teasers, title and one-sentence outcome, **not linked** (write-ups don't exist yet; design should degrade gracefully). Space for 2–3 future testimonials that looks intentional while empty.

### 5.6 Good questions (FAQ content)
- How will I know where things stand? (Working files, written rationale, recorded walkthroughs, weekly written update: done, next, what I need from you. Calls whenever they'd genuinely help.)
- Will this fit our stack? (Standards-based: DTCG tokens, Style Dictionary outputs to whatever platforms you ship, plain CSS variables if that's your stack. Your engineers see the pipeline before handoff; the docs are written for them.)
- What if my project doesn't fit an engagement? (Intake anyway. Scoping is free, and if it isn't a fit I'll say so honestly.)
- What do you need from me to start? (Access, brand assets, a decision-maker who responds within a day.)
- How do revisions work? (One structured revision round in every engagement, defined in the scope doc.)
- Who does the work? (One senior designer. No juniors, no outsourcing.)
- Do you work with agencies? (Yes, white-label. Same engagements, your client relationship.)

### 5.7 Start an intake
Fields: name · email · company/product link (optional) · which problem brought you here (pre-filled by the selector; editable; includes "something else") · "What are you building, and what's in the way?" (free text) · timeline · "How did you find Ideacamp?" · agency/white-label checkbox. Under the submit: "Scope, price, and start date within 2 business days." Supporting bullets: no sales call required · agencies welcome. Include spam protection that is invisible to humans and assistive tech.

### 5.8 Engagement details page (separate, footer-linked, and linked from the credit note)
Plain language, not legalese. Contents: the audit credit policy (Diagnose fee credits in full toward Build the Foundation or Design the Product if booked within 60 days of audit delivery; one credit per engagement; no cash value; applies to Diagnose only) · what a revision round includes · payment terms (50% deposit reserves the start date, balance at handoff) · how scope changes are handled (a written change to the scope doc, agreed before work continues) · deliverable ownership (transfers to the client on final payment; pre-existing tools and methods remain Ideacamp's) · confidentiality (client work is never shown without permission) · a note that the signed services agreement is the binding document · a short privacy note covering intake-form data (what's collected, that it's used only to respond, never sold).

### Navigation
Problems (or the selector) · Engagements · Process · About · Questions · Start an intake. Footer: wordmark, intake link, contact email, engagement details link.

## 6. Design latitude

The designer owns: page structure expression, information hierarchy, visual identity, typography, color, imagery approach, motion, component patterns, the selector's form, and how transparency is expressed. Intent to serve: precise, quietly confident, craft-forward — a storefront a design systems engineer would inspect and respect, and a busy founder could buy from in under three minutes. Distinctiveness is welcome; restraint on visuals in this brief is delegation, not a request for blandness.

## 7. Constraints (hard requirements)

- Content, claims, prices, and problem language as specified; no hourly rates; no urgency mechanics; no fictional plural team.
- The owner's name may appear in the about content, but the brand is Ideacamp. No mention of any current or past employer as an employer of the owner beyond the credentials listed. No social links required.
- The selector never gates content; full static content path required.
- Accessible (WCAG AA minimum), fast, responsive. Any repeated/decorative content hidden from assistive tech; motion respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Launch scope: the main page, the engagement details page, the intake form. No blog, newsletter, or chat.
- Built cleanly enough to survive inspection by a design systems engineer (semantic structure, tokenized styling), because it will be inspected.

## 8. Success criteria

A founder can land, recognize their problem, see the recommended engagement and price, and submit an intake in under three minutes without a call. A design leader finds precise, stack-credible language for their systems problem. An agency principal doing diligence on an outreach email finds a polished, credible operation in one scroll. A design systems engineer views source and nods.
